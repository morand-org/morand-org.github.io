The answer hinges on whether any primary-care or sports-medicine cohorts report diagnosis-specific proportions with a neck-pain consultation denominator, rather than population incidence or specialty-clinic prevalence. Let me check the data for the mechanical, traumatic/sports, neurological/serious and inflammatory diagnoses in parallel.

Still open is whether any source gives age-banded or sex-specific diagnostic shares within neck-pain consulters, or a sports-medicine clinic denominator; let me check those remaining gaps directly.

**None of the 17 age curves can be confirmed as drawn.** Only one primary-care dataset reports diagnostic labels with a neck-complaint denominator: the Australian POLAR general-practice database (14,492 patients, 269 practices, 2014–2018). It is restricted to age ≥45 (mean 61.9 years) and has no age-band or sex breakdown within the neck diagnoses.[1] No neck-pain-denominator data were found for ages 12–17 or 18–39, for a 40–59 versus 60+ split, or for any sports-medicine clinic population.

## Reading the table

- **POLAR percentages are shares of diagnostic labels, not of patients.** About 27% of patients carried two or more neck labels, so patient-level proportions would be somewhat higher.[1]
- **Labels are what the GP recorded, not verified diagnoses.** Most were non-specific "pain" or "ache" labels.[1]
- **The 17 categories are not mutually exclusive.** Direct pathoanatomical causes of mechanical neck pain are rarely identifiable, so F2, F3 and F4 overlap heavily with F1.[41]
- **Overall consultation sex ratio:** new neck-symptom consultations in Dutch general practice were 23.1 per 1,000 person-years and more frequent in women; no diagnosis-specific split was retrieved.[42]
- **Rows F11–F16 are absent from POLAR's published list.** That list shows only labels making up at least 1% of neck diagnoses, so these conditions cannot be quantified from it.[1]
