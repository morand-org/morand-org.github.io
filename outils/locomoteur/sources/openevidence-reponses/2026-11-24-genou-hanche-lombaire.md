Each of these findings needs a study that measured that specific sign or symptom against a reference standard for the stated diagnosis, and several of these overuse and bursal conditions may never have been studied this way. Let me check the diagnostic accuracy literature for each group: ligament and patellar findings, the overuse and apophyseal conditions, crystal and septic arthritis, and popliteal cyst.  
  
  
  
**Only 6 of the 22 codes have published sensitivity and specificity (P4, P16, P17, P19, P21, P25), 2 have sensitivity only (P5, P20), and 14 have no data.** Where data exist, most current values are too optimistic, especially P4, P17 and P19. Likelihood ratios marked "calc." are arithmetic from the published sensitivity and specificity or 2×2 counts, not figures the authors reported.

**Verdict on the current values**

\- **P4 (current LR 5): correct downward.** Isolated valgus laxity in primary care gives an LR+ of about 1.8; its value is in ruling out (LR− about 0.18). Only the combination of history, pain and laxity on valgus stress reached an LR+ of 6.4.<u>[\[2\]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781119413936.ch137)[\[1\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18954845)</u>  
  
- **P5 (current LR 5): unsupported.** The only figure is a sensitivity of about 25% from a small emergency series, with no specificity.<u>[\[3\]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11557647/)[\[4\]](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546507312641)</u>  
  
- **P16 (current 6 / 0.4): published values are more favorable (about 8 / 0.11).** They come from arthroscopy populations. The largest primary study used controls with lateral joint-line pain, which likely inflates specificity.<u>[\[16\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24280039)[\[24\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18063174)</u>  
  
- **P17 (current 6 / 1): correct both.** Patient-reported gout gave LR+ 2.0 and LR− 0.56 in primary care. In rheumatology clinics, self-reported gout had sensitivity 80% and specificity 72% (LR+ about 2.9, LR− about 0.28, calc.).<u>[\[17\]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/10.1001/archinternmed.2010.196?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[25\]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acr.22896?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)</u>  
  
- **P19 (current 5 / 0.4): LR+ far too high, LR− about right.** Redness was present in 76% of non-gout monoarthritis in the Janssens cohort, giving an LR+ of 1.2.[<u>\[17\]</u>](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/10.1001/archinternmed.2010.196?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- **P21 (current 5 / 1): correct to 3.1 / 0.73.** A recalculation in the emergency medicine literature gives the same point estimates with CIs of 1.9–4.5 and 0.55–0.88.<u>[\[19\]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/10.1001/jama.297.13.1478?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[26\]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781119616870.ch32)</u>  
  
- **P25 (current 8 / 0.4): compatible with the pooled 6.3 / 0.30,** whose confidence intervals are wide.[<u>\[23\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41939773)  
  
- **P20 (current 4 / 0.2): unsupported.** No specificity exists.[<u>\[19\]</u>](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/10.1001/jama.297.13.1478?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- **P22 and P23: unsupported.** The JAMA Rational Clinical Examination review found no examination finding other than fever had been studied for septic arthritis.[<u>\[19\]</u>](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/10.1001/jama.297.13.1478?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)

**Caveats that affect use of these numbers**

\- **Comparator mismatch (P17, P19).** All Janssens patients already carried a GP diagnosis of gout, so the non-gout group was other inflammatory monoarthritis. Against a general knee pain population the specificity of redness would differ, but no study measures this.[<u>\[17\]</u>](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/10.1001/archinternmed.2010.196?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- **Target mismatch (P4, P5, P17–P19, P21).** Valgus data cover MCL only and varus data LCL only. The gout data are not knee-specific and no item-level data exist for CPP arthritis. The prosthesis figure comes from a risk-factor cohort, not patients presenting with a painful knee.<u>[\[1\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18954845)[\[4\]](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546507312641)[\[17\]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/10.1001/archinternmed.2010.196?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[18\]](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.42619?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[20\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8849354)</u>  
  
- **P25 conflicts with primary studies.** Clinical examination detected only 2 of 10 ultrasound-confirmed cysts in one rheumatoid arthritis series and 29 of 67 in another. The questionnaire item is a reported mass, whereas the meta-analysis pooled examiner findings.<u>[\[27\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12734890)[\[28\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8575143)[\[23\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41939773)</u>

**Nearest evidence for the "no data" codes**

\- **P6:** in 39 acute patellar instability cases, 19 described a subluxation episode, with no comparison group.[<u>\[5\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32488565)  
  
- **P7:** a 2022 review states that even the Noble compression test has unknown likelihood ratios.[<u>\[6\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01634-3)  
  
- **P10–P11:** clinically diagnosed pes anserine tendinobursitis rarely shows ultrasound abnormality (1 tendinitis and 2 bursitis among 37 patients in one series; 2 of 26 in another). MRI-confirmed bursitis most often presented with medial joint-line pain rather than pain below it.<u>[\[9\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10898072)[\[11\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15716614)[\[10\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15940489)</u>  
  
- **P12–P15:** tenderness is part of the clinical case definition for Osgood-Schlatter disease, so imaging studies cannot yield unbiased accuracy. Evidence for Sinding-Larsen-Johansson disease is mainly case studies.<u>[\[13\]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12178-021-09708-5)[\[14\]](https://doi.org/10.1542/pir.2023-006037)[\[15\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39346060)</u>  
  
- **P20, P22:** clinical effusion had a multivariable odds ratio of 4.8 (1.2–20.0) for native knee septic arthritis in one emergency series. Redness was more frequent in septic arthritis on univariate analysis only in another.<u>[\[29\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33280970)[\[21\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25819038)</u>  
  
- **P24:** 2 of 19 MRI-detected popliteal cysts had pain at the cyst site.[<u>\[22\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.11254?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
Three PMIDs (Janssens 2010, Margaretten 2007, and the primary varus study) did not appear in the retrieved records and are left blank rather than supplied from memory.

**GENO-P1: Clarke's test**

\- **Values confirmed, target wrong.** The four figures match Doberstein 2008 exactly, but that study measured chondromalacia at arthroscopy in people selected for having no patellofemoral pain.[<u>\[1\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18345345)  
  
- **Against a patellofemoral pain reference: no data retrieved.** Arthroscopic chondromalacia severity was not associated with anterior knee pain in a separate cohort (p = 0.83), so the two targets are not interchangeable.[<u>\[4\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20360517)  
  
- **One lead to check.** Nijs 2006 (Man Ther) is the likely primary study of Clarke's test against clinical patellofemoral pain; it could not be retrieved, so no values are entered.  
  
- **Systematic review.** The Cook 2012 review covered 22 clinical tests from 9 studies, only 1 of high quality; its abstract does not say whether Clarke's test was included.[<u>\[5\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22507358)

**GENO-P2: true locking**

\- **Current LR+ 2 / LR− 0.8 not traceable.** The nearest published figure is the LR+ of 1.78 in the table.[<u>\[2\]</u>](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acr.20354?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- **Definition mismatch.** The Niu item asks whether the knee "gets stuck temporarily so that you can't move it further", not about inability to extend.[<u>\[2\]</u>](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acr.20354?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- **Checklist word "locking" alone performed worse.** Sensitivity was 33% (25–42), specificity 73% (66–79), and LR+ 1.23 (0.87–1.74).[<u>\[2\]</u>](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acr.20354?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- **LR− by calculation only.** Sensitivity and specificity give about 0.72 for the expanded item and 0.92 for the checklist word; neither is a published value.  
  
- **Orthopedic alternative.** Yan 2011 (Swiss Med Wkly, PMID 22180191) reported sensitivity 55.2% and specificity 96% against arthroscopy in 262 knees. Calculated LR+ is about 13.8 and LR− about 0.47; spectrum and verification bias are likely in this surgical series.[<u>\[6\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22180191)  
  
- **Closest match to the French wording.** In 817 arthroscopy patients, self-reported inability to straighten the knee had a prevalence ratio of 1.02 (0.84–1.23) for meniscal tear, with no sensitivity or specificity reported.[<u>\[7\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30170997)  
  
- **Primary care.** No locking-specific accuracy was retrieved from the Wagemakers 2008, Snoeker 2015, or Décary 2018 studies.<u>[\[8\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18185035)[\[9\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26212848)[\[10\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29111463)</u>  
  
- **Review-level range.** A 38-study review gave sensitivity 0.32–0.69 and specificity 0.45–0.74 for catching and locking combined, without pooling.[<u>\[11\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34600120)

**GENO-P3: inferior pole tenderness**

\- **Current LR+ 1.8 / LR− 0.4 unverified.** Neither study abstract below reports sensitivity, specificity, or likelihood ratios; the full texts are needed.  
  
- **Cook 2001.** Positive predictive value was 68% in symptomatic tendons and 36–38% as screening in asymptomatic athletes; the authors called palpation moderately sensitive but not specific.[<u>\[3\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11157466)

- **Quadriceps tendinopathy: no data.** The inferior pole is the patellar tendon origin, so this finding does not localize to the quadriceps tendon.[<u>\[13\]</u>](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013078.pub2/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)

**How to read the table**

\- **The main adult anchor is weak.** The Jackson figures are pooled estimates from a review of acute knee pain in adults, with no age or sex breakdown.[<u>\[7\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-139-7-200310070-00010)  
  
- **Coded-diagnosis figures understate specific diagnoses.** In the Australian database, 26.4% of knee labels were the nonspecific "knee pain" and 19.0% were knee replacement.[<u>\[8\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-023-01976-z)  
  
- **Referral cohorts overstate structural diagnoses.** The paediatric knee cohort allowed more than one diagnosis per patient, and the sports-clinic series skew young and male.<u>[\[12\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3538273)[\[4\]](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546510383002)</u>  
  
- **Primary care and sports medicine should not share one curve.** Patellofemoral pain alone differs about twofold between the two settings.[<u>\[1\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26834209)  
  
- **Imaging proxies are not clinical diagnoses.** Baker's cysts on MRI were about as common in asymptomatic as symptomatic osteoarthritic knees (20.8% vs 23.4%).[<u>\[51\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.11254?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- **The Dutch cohort could not fill the adolescent gap.** HONEUR enrolled 1,068 consulters aged 12 and over, but its diagnosis-by-age tables were not in the retrieved text.[<u>\[52\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16117830)  
  
Would it help to rebuild the curves from age- and sex-specific population incidence data (Skåne, Olmsted County, Dutch general practice registries), with the denominator stated for each?

**Corrections to the three codes with data**

\- **P13 (LR+ 6, LR− 0.7):** The only published estimate puts snapping at LR+ ≈1.3 and LR− ≈0.93 against injection response, and no finding in that study was significantly associated with response. A 2026 systematic review found only three diagnostic studies of iliopsoas-related groin pain, all at high risk of bias.<u>[\[6\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33340679)[\[25\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42590015)</u>  
  
- **P24 (LR+ 5, LR− 0.8):** The composite has not been studied. Component LR+ values range from 2.3 to 7.7 with wide intervals, and component LR− values of 0.57–0.95 are broadly consistent with the current 0.8. The abstract's "LR ∞" for the femoral stretch test and patellar reflex reflects zero false positives in a small sample, and a secondary review tabulates different patellar reflex figures (0.32/0.90) for the same study, so the full text needs checking.<u>[\[21\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20543768)[\[22\]](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-017-1549-6)[\[26\]](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-016-1383-2)</u>  
  
- **P26 (LR 5):** No study tests a 30-minute threshold. At \>45 minutes, the calculated LR+ is about 1.8 against all comparators, about 3.9 against shoulder conditions, and about 1.1 against rheumatoid arthritis.[<u>\[23\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.34356?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)

**Nearest published proxies (not the coded finding)**

These are different findings or reference standards and should not be substituted without relabeling the item.  
  
- **P10:** Greater trochanter palpation tenderness (not pain location) had 80% sensitivity for MRI tendinopathy in 65 patients with lateral hip pain; specificity was not retrievable. In 46 women it had sensitivity 85.7%, specificity 61.1%, LR+ 2.2 (1.2–4.0), and LR− 0.2 (0.1–0.6), but against clinical case status rather than imaging.<u>[\[1\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27633027)[\[27\]](https://doi.org/10.1089/jwh.2016.5889)</u>  
  
- **P12:** In 83 footballers with posterior thigh injury, tenderness was present in all 83 whether or not MRI confirmed a hamstring strain (68 confirmed, 15 not), so it did not discriminate in that cohort.[<u>\[4\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465030310063701)  
  
- **P15:** The item describes provocative pressure at the nerve, for which no accuracy study was found. The pelvic compression test is a different, symptom-relieving maneuver with reported sensitivity 95% and specificity 93.3% in a retrospective neurosurgical series of 45 patients, without confidence intervals or an explicit reference standard. That study was published in Neurosurgery (2007, PMID 17415207).<u>[\[8\]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40122-024-00693-4)[\[28\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17415207)</u>  
  
- **P17:** Pubic-related groin pain is defined by symphysis tenderness, so accuracy against that diagnosis is circular. Against MRI bone marrow oedema, counts from 89 footballers give a pain-plus-tenderness composite sensitivity of 80.4% and specificity of 76.7% (calculated, not author-reported). Marrow oedema is also common in asymptomatic players.<u>[\[9\]](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-13847-1)[\[11\]](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2004.00380.x?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[29\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25512059)</u>  
  
- **P25 and P27:** Every participant in the classification cohort had bilateral shoulder pain, so shoulder involvement could not be tested. Neck aching did not discriminate (57% of PMR vs 54% of comparators).[<u>\[23\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.34356?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)

**Items resting on expert opinion only**

\- **P20 and P21:** Examination findings in slipped capital femoral epiphysis are rated as expert opinion, symmetric hip range of motion can occur at any slip severity, and the Drehmann sign also occurs in other hip pathology. The LR of 0.2 for full painless internal rotation therefore has no empirical support as a rule-out.[<u>\[17\]</u>](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2025/1000/slipped-capital-femoral-epiphysis)  
  
- **P18, P22, P23:** Large avulsion series enrolled only radiograph-positive cases, and iliac crest apophysitis evidence is limited to case series and reviews.<u>[\[12\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11357449)[\[13\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25321882)[\[19\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/998842)</u>  
  
- **P19:** The only accuracy study in proximal hamstring tendinopathy tested three stretch provocation tests, not pain location.[<u>\[16\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22219215)  
  
PMIDs for several supporting studies (Verrall 2003 and 2005, Ganderton 2017) were not retrievable and are omitted rather than guessed.

**Verdict on each current value**

\- **P1 (LR 1.6):** Not confirmed. The JAMA Rational Clinical Examination review (Metcalfe, 2019; PMID not verified) reports only morning stiffness under 60 minutes: sensitivity 56–91%, specificity 41–67%, LR+ 1.5–1.7, LR− 0.22–0.65, in 415 hips against radiographic osteoarthritis. The current 1.6 matches that 60-minute range, not a 30-minute threshold.[<u>\[1\]</u>](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/10.1001/jama.2019.19413?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- **P2 (LR 0.8):** Not confirmed. The only related figure is the LR− of 0.22–0.65 for absence of "morning stiffness under 60 minutes", which is lower than 0.8. Whether "absent" meant no stiffness or stiffness beyond 60 minutes is not stated in the retrieved text.[<u>\[1\]</u>](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/10.1001/jama.2019.19413?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- **P3 (LR+ 0.3, LR− 1.2):** Not confirmed. Fearon (Br J Sports Med, 2013, PMID 22983121) reports only an odds ratio of 0.06 (95% CI 0.00–0.26) for shoe-and-sock difficulty in greater trochanteric pain syndrome versus hip osteoarthritis. The direction agrees, but the current values imply an odds ratio of 0.25, and the comparison is against hip osteoarthritis only.[<u>\[2\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22983121)  
  
- **P4 (LR+ 1.6, LR− 0.3):** Plausible but not confirmed. Ganderton gives 2.2 and 0.2 in a case-control sample, which probably inflates accuracy. Grimaldi supports 80% sensitivity against MRI in consecutive lateral hip pain patients. The Kinsella meta-analysis is the preferred source and should be checked for the pooled palpation estimate.<u>[\[3\]](https://doi.org/10.1089/jwh.2016.5889)[\[4\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27633027)[\[5\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37561820)</u>  
  
- **P5 (LR+ 3, LR− 0.15):** No source found. Serner (Br J Sports Med, 2016, PMID 27531521) reports only a negative predictive value of 91–96% for palpation predicting a negative MRI in 81 athletes with acute groin injury. The Doha classification defines adductor-related groin pain as adductor tenderness plus pain on resisted adduction, so both P5 and P6 are part of the target definition.<u>[\[6\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27531521)[\[12\]](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-13847-1)</u>  
  
- **P6 (LR+ 4, LR− 0.5):** Likely taken from a different target. Verrall (Scand J Med Sci Sports, 2005) found squeeze-test sensitivity 49% and specificity 88% for pubic bone stress injury in 89 Australian footballers. Those figures calculate to LR+ about 4.2 and LR− about 0.58 (not published by the authors). Falvey (Br J Sports Med, 2016, PMID 26626272) reports 85.4% sensitivity for combined pubic aponeurosis and adductor pathology, with a stated "negative likelihood ratio 1.95" that looks mislabelled.<u>[\[13\]](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2004.00380.x?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[7\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26626272)</u>  
  
- **P7 (LR+ 2.5, LR− 0.2):** LR+ not supported. A patient-history review (Wright, Arch Phys Med Rehabil, 2021, PMID 33930328) gives LR+ 2.51–3.86 for groin or anterior thigh pain in hip osteoarthritis, which may be the origin of 2.5. The LR− of 0.13 comes from a cohort where 78 of 79 hips were diseased, so only the sensitivity is usable. Martin (Arthroscopy, 2008, PMID 18760208) found groin pain not useful for identifying intra-articular pain by injection response.<u>[\[14\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33930328)[\[8\]](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12651?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[15\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18760208)</u>  
  
- **P8 (LR+ 2, LR− 0.8):** No source found. Narvani's 100%/85% implies LR+ about 6.7 and LR− 0, but rests on four tears. Martin found clicking not useful. No study assessed the combined click, catch or lock item, and the meta-analysis by Reiman (Br J Sports Med, 2015, PMID 25515771) pooled examination tests only.<u>[\[9\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12897984)[\[15\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18760208)[\[16\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25515771)</u>  
  
- **P9 (LR+ 5, LR− 0.8):** No source found. Corticosteroid evidence is etiologic: odds ratio 1.65 (95% CI 1.51–1.80) for any prior oral corticosteroid in a Taiwanese case-control study. Glucocorticoid use is reported in 25–50% of nontraumatic cases. The fluoroquinolone literature retrieved describes tendinopathy, tendon rupture and arthralgia, not osteonecrosis, so pairing the two exposures in one item is not supported.<u>[\[10\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32011437)[\[11\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30348552)[\[17\]](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-93763-y)[\[18\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21333952)</u>  
  
Would you like the full-text tables of the Kinsella meta-analysis and Metcalfe eTable 4 pursued, since they hold the pooled palpation and per-study morning stiffness figures?

**Where the evidence disagrees with the current estimates**

\- **F6 (impingement or labral tear):** The 3–8% estimate for ages 15–45 is well below every presenter-based figure: 17% confirmed in Dutch general practice and 38% in the population-based cohort.[<u>\[17\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546520970914)  
  
- **F7 (iliopsoas):** The estimate has no sex modifier, but the one mixed-sex clinic showed female predominance (17% of female vs 6% of male diagnoses).[<u>\[9\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546515582031)  
  
- **F2 (trochanteric pain):** The sex direction is supported; the published case ratio is about 4:1, and community data showed no age effect between 50 and 79.<u>[\[6\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15808035)[\[8\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17678660)</u>  
  
- **F3, F10, F11:** Sports clinic sex contrasts are far larger than the estimated multipliers.[<u>\[9\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546515582031)  
  
- **F12 and F17:** The paediatric sports clinic figures (about 10% and 11.5%) are higher than the estimates, but they pool ages 5–17 and F17 covers all pelvic sites.[<u>\[18\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546514522393)

**Caveats on the figures in the table**

\- **Overlapping entities:** Groin entities are not mutually exclusive (multiple entities in 33–47% of athletes), so sports clinic percentages sum to more than 100%.<u>[\[11\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17261557)[\[19\]](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-13847-1)</u>  
  
- **Rankin denominators:** The reported percentages (adductor 33%, pubic 28%) do not equal the counts divided by 894, and the sex-specific figures are per diagnosis, not per patient.[<u>\[9\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546515582031)  
  
- **Paediatric data:** Counts are back-calculated from percentages in a dancer-heavy referral clinic.[<u>\[18\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546514522393)  
  
- **Selected cohorts:** Emergency, MRI-selected and tertiary figures (F5, F16, F19, F20) will overstate primary care proportions.<u>[\[32\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22623574)[\[15\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15805330)[\[36\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26993257)</u>

**LOMB‑P5: pregnancy or recent delivery**

No study was found that measured this item against pregnancy-related low back or pelvic girdle pain. Accuracy is hard to measure in the usual way because pregnancy or recent delivery is part of the case definition, with onset described from the first trimester to 3 weeks postpartum.[<u>\[2\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15338362)  
  
- **Sensitivity** is near 100% by construction, not by measurement. It falls only if "récent" excludes women with persisting pain; 8.6% still had pain 2 years after delivery in one cohort.[<u>\[6\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11380285)  
  
- **Specificity** depends on how many pregnant or postpartum women in the questionnaire population have back pain from another cause. No study gives that figure.  
  
- **Not every pregnant woman with back pain meets the definition.** The European guideline requires exclusion of lumbar causes and reproduction of pain by specific tests. In one cohort, 17% of lumbopelvic pain in pregnancy was lumbar-only.<u>[\[1\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18259783)[\[7\]](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD012441/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[8\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16508539)</u>  
  
- **Arithmetic check:** LR+ 200 with LR− 0.01 implies specificity of 99.5%. That is a case-mix assumption, not a published value.

**LOMB‑P6: pain radiating below the knee**

Konstantinou 2012 is the only study found that tested this exact self-reported item. It was the best single item, but its AUC of 0.67–0.68 fell short of acceptable discrimination.[<u>\[3\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22752591) The provisional LR+ 6 / LR− 0.2 corresponds to sensitivity 0.83 and specificity 0.86, an AUC of about 0.84. The study's reference standard was clinical nerve root involvement, not disc herniation, so the comparison is indirect. The exact values are in the full-text tables of that paper.

- **"Sciatica" for disc herniation:** sensitivity 95%, specificity 88%, as quoted in a 1998 JAMA article. These give LR+ 7.9 and LR− 0.06 by arithmetic. They are usually attributed to Deyo, JAMA 1992 (PMID not verified). The original table was not retrieved, so it is unclear whether they were measured or estimated. The ACP/APS guideline calls the specificity of a sciatica history uncertain.<u>[\[9\]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/10.1001/jama.280.8.730?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[10\]](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-147-7-200710020-00006)</u>  
  
- **Dermatomal pain distribution (Vroomen, J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry, 2002, PMID 11971050):** sensitivity 0.89 (0.84–0.93), specificity 0.31 (0.24–0.40), LR+ 1.3 (1.1–1.5), LR− 0.34 (0.20–0.58). This was in 274 primary-care patients against MRI nerve root compression, as recalculated by Petersen 2017. All patients already had leg-radiating pain, so this is a different question.<u>[\[11\]](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-017-1549-6)[\[12\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11971050)</u>  
  
- **Stynes, PLoS One (PMID 29621243):** below-knee pain was retained in both primary-care diagnostic models, but no single-item accuracy is given in the abstract.[<u>\[13\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29621243)  
  
- **Verwoerd, Spine J, 2014 (PMID 24325881):** all 395 patients had radicular leg pain, so radiation could not be tested.[<u>\[14\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24325881)  
  
- **Meta-analyses:** the Vroomen 1999 review (PMID 10552236) found pain distribution the only useful history item but reports no pooled values in its abstract. The Cochrane review (PMID 20166095) covered physical examination only.<u>[\[15\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10552236)[\[16\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20166095)</u>

**LOMB‑P7: visible scoliosis or rib hump**

No study was found that measured inspection, rib hump or the forward bend test against standing radiographs in adult degenerative scoliosis. A 2026 JAMA letter states that deformity in adult symptomatic lumbar scoliosis cannot be reliably assessed on examination and needs standing radiographs; that is expert opinion.[<u>\[4\]</u>](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/10.1001/jama.2026.18623?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral) The nearest adult study (Pérennou, Spine, 1994, PMID 8153817) gives only prevalence: lumbar scoliosis in 7.5% of adults with low back pain and 15% of those over 60.[<u>\[5\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8153817)  
  
Accuracy data exist only for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis screening, a different population:  
  
- **Karachalios, Spine, 1999 (PMID 10586455):** forward bend test alone had sensitivity 84.4% (67.2–94.7) and specificity 95.2% (94.3–95.9) for Cobb ≥10° in 2,700 pupils aged 8–16, per the USPSTF recalculation.<u>[\[17\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10586455)[\[18\]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/10.1001/jama.2017.11669?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)</u>  
  
- **Fong, Spine, 2010 (PMID 20393399):** this meta-analysis of 36 school-screening studies found too few data to pool sensitivity or specificity.[<u>\[19\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20393399)  
  
These figures should not be transferred to adults. Degenerative curves are mostly lumbar, and inter-examiner agreement on the forward bend test was poor in the lumbar spine.<u>[\[20\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31305254)[\[21\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9563110)</u>  
  
Would it help to look at the accuracy of combined history-and-examination models for radiculopathy (Stynes score, RAPIDH criteria), which may suit the questionnaire better than single-item likelihood ratios?

**Verdict on each current value**

**LOMB-P1 (current LR+ 5, LR− 0.9).**  
  
- The LR− of 0.9 is consistent with the corticosteroid data (0.88).[<u>\[1\]</u>](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD014461.pub2/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- The LR+ of 5 matches no study. The primary-care estimates are 2.46 in adults over 55 and 48.50 (11.62–165.22) in an all-age Australian cohort with only 8 fractures.<u>[\[1\]](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD014461.pub2/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[8\]](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.24853?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)</u>  
  
- The Cochrane reviewers did not pool; the primary-care range is LR+ 2.46 to 48.50 and LR− 0.75 to 0.99.[<u>\[9\]</u>](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cca/doi/10.1002/cca.4399/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- The studies tested prolonged or unspecified corticosteroid use, not recent use.[<u>\[1\]</u>](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD014461.pub2/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- No accuracy study of fluoroquinolone exposure for vertebral fracture was found; the retrieved fluoroquinolone harm literature concerns tendon and other collagen-related events.[<u>\[10\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-019-00828-z)  
  
**LOMB-P2 (current LR 4).**  
  
- The closest match for osteoporotic fracture is LR+ 6.42 with LR− 0.82.[<u>\[1\]</u>](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD014461.pub2/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- The all-age primary-care cohort gave LR+ 10.03 (2.76–26.36) and LR− 0.77 (0.42–0.95), with sensitivity 25% and specificity 98%.[<u>\[8\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.24853?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- That cohort counted minor trauma in elderly patients as positive, which differs from the questionnaire's high-energy wording.[<u>\[8\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.24853?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- The primary-care range is LR+ 1.93 to 12.85 and LR− 0.37 to 0.88.[<u>\[9\]</u>](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cca/doi/10.1002/cca.4399/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- In emergency departments, trauma history is much less informative (LR+ 0.18 to 1.93).[<u>\[11\]</u>](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD008643.pub3/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
**LOMB-P3 (current LR+ 1.5, LR− 0.8).**  
  
- These values could not be verified. The only study designed to test the manoeuvre concluded it was neither sensitive nor specific, and its per-side 2×2 tables were not retrievable.[<u>\[4\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16980534)  
  
- Secondary sources disagree on specificity: 17–32% in one review and 0–87% in a systematic review of 8 athlete studies.<u>[\[5\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27317501)[\[6\]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2016.3334?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)</u>  
  
- As a consistency check only (not a value to enter): sensitivity 50–73% with specificity 17–32% bounds LR+ at roughly 0.6–1.07, below the current 1.5.[<u>\[6\]</u>](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2016.3334?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- The data apply to active spondylolysis only. No stork-test accuracy data were found for isthmic spondylolisthesis.[<u>\[5\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27317501)  
  
- For spondylolisthesis, step-off palpation has data: sensitivity 0.60, specificity 0.87, LR+ 4.7 (1.6–13.9), LR− 0.46 (0.16–1.35) in one study.[<u>\[12\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-017-1549-6)  
  
**LOMB-P4 (current LR+ 3, LR− 0.5).**  
  
- The LR− of 0.5 agrees with the Deyo-derived figure.[<u>\[7\]</u>](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM200009073431008?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- The LR+ of 3 does not; the same source gives 26.[<u>\[7\]</u>](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM200009073431008?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- The Deyo-derived figures combine sensitivity and specificity from different populations and have no confidence intervals.[<u>\[7\]</u>](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM200009073431008?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- A value near 3 appears only as an adjusted odds ratio of 2.8 (1.3–6.0) for fever in an emergency cohort already suspected of infection. That is not a likelihood ratio.[<u>\[13\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31128933)  
  
- A systematic review of red flags for spinal infection could not assess diagnostic accuracy at all.[<u>\[14\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-019-2949-6)  
  
- The main primary-care cohort had zero infections among 1,172 patients.[<u>\[8\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.24853?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- Sensitivity varies by infection type: fever occurs in up to 45% of bacterial vertebral osteomyelitis, about 50% of spondylodiscitis, and 24–62% of epidural abscess.<u>[\[15\]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMra2412728?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[16\]](https://academic.oup.com/cid/article-lookup/doi/10.1093/cid/civ482)[\[17\]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-019-04393-6)</u>  
  
- No back-pain study reporting chills separately was found.

**Limits of this verification**

\- The Cochrane review rated all primary-care fracture studies at high or unclear risk of bias for the reference standard and for flow and timing.[<u>\[9\]</u>](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cca/doi/10.1002/cca.4399/full?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)  
  
- PMIDs are given only where the retrieved record confirmed them. Those for Han 2023, Henschke 2009, Deyo 1992 and Lurie 2000 need a separate lookup.  
  
- The full-text tables of Masci 2006 are the missing piece for LOMB-P3; the side-specific sensitivity and specificity there would settle whether 1.5 and 0.8 are defensible.  
  
Would it help to look at combined red-flag rules for vertebral fracture next, such as the Henschke rule (≥2 of 4 features, LR+ 15.5), as a replacement for the two single items?

**Corrections that matter most**

\- **Non-specific LBP (F1):** primary-care sources put it at more than 85% to about 90%. These figures do not separate strain, discogenic, facet or sacroiliac pain, because clinical tests do not reliably attribute pain to those structures. The comparable quantity is therefore the sum of F1–F4 and F7, not F1 alone.<u>[\[5\]](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-147-7-200710020-00006)[\[46\]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/10.1001/jama.2026.9631?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)[\[47\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27745712)</u>  
  
- **Pain-generator rows (F3, F4, F7):** the published proportions come from chronic, referred patients who had diagnostic blocks or discography. The sacroiliac consensus guideline states that its rates do not estimate prevalence in the overall LBP population.<u>[\[1\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37096189)[\[21\]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12681192/)</u>  
  
- **Spondylolysis (F11):** the adolescent proportion depends on setting, from under 1% in claims data to 30% in a sports-medicine clinic. A single curve for "primary care or sports medicine" cannot represent both.<u>[\[2\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26368854)[\[31\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31460956)</u>  
  
- **Sex multipliers:** the F9 multipliers imply a female-to-male ratio of 6.25:1, while population studies report 1.3:1 to about 3:1. The F11 multipliers imply 1.6:1, below the 2.2–3.5:1 seen in symptomatic adolescents. The F13 male excess is not seen in primary-care chronic LBP.<u>[\[25\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19139672)[\[24\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17202902)[\[26\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24126641)[\[34\]](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-023-06679-1)[\[35\]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35504566)[\[37\]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acr.22180?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)</u>  
  
- **Degenerative scoliosis (F12):** the only LBP-population series reports 2% under 45 and 15% over 60, above the current 1–4%.[<u>\[36\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8153817)  
  
- **Axial spondyloarthritis (F13):** the 24% Dutch figure has not been replicated.[<u>\[48\]</u>](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMcp2032396?utm_source=openevidence&utm_medium=referral)

**Limits of this evidence**

Several widely quoted figures (fracture 4%, stenosis 3%, herniated disc 4%, infection 0.01%, cauda equina 0.04%) are guideline estimates without numerators.[<u>\[5\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-147-7-200710020-00006) The 47% and 5% spondylolysis figures for adolescent and adult athletes were retrieved only through secondary citation.[<u>\[33\]</u>](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12178-022-09760-9) Radiographic spondylolisthesis was not associated with LBP in community studies, so imaging prevalence overstates it as a cause.[<u>\[25\]</u>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19139672)  
  
Would it help to look next at how the claims-based adolescent data and the sports-medicine MRI series could be reconciled into setting-specific priors for spondylolysis?
